General Borehole Questions

What is a borehole and how does it work?

A borehole is a narrow shaft drilled into the ground to access underground water. The process involves:

  • Drilling through soil and rock layers to reach the water table
  • Installing casing to prevent collapse and contamination
  • Adding a screen to filter out sediment
  • Installing a pump system to bring water to the surface
  • Connecting to your property's water system

Boreholes provide a sustainable, independent water source for residential, agricultural, or commercial use.

How deep should a borehole be?

Borehole depth varies significantly based on location and geology:

  • Shallow boreholes: 15-50 meters (for seasonal water)
  • Average residential: 50-150 meters
  • Commercial/Agricultural: 100-300 meters
  • Deep boreholes: 300+ meters (in arid regions)

We conduct geophysical surveys to determine optimal depth before drilling. Deeper isn't always better - the goal is to reach sustainable aquifers with good water quality.

Do I need permission to drill a borehole?

Yes, borehole drilling requires proper authorization in South Africa:

  • Water Use License: Required for large-scale extraction
  • Municipal Permission: Needed in urban areas
  • Environmental Assessment: For sensitive areas
  • Geological Survey: To identify optimal drilling points

We handle all permit applications and regulatory compliance as part of our service. The process typically takes 4-8 weeks depending on your location and the scale of the project.

How long does borehole drilling take?

The timeline varies based on several factors:

  • Site assessment & permits: 1-2 weeks
  • Actual drilling: 2-5 days for a typical residential borehole
  • Casing & development: 1-2 days
  • Pump installation: 1-2 days
  • Water testing & commissioning: 1 week

Total project completion typically takes 3-6 weeks from start to finish. Complex projects or difficult geological conditions may extend this timeline.

Technical Questions

What types of pumps are available?

We offer several pump options based on your needs:

  • Submersible pumps: Most common, placed inside the borehole
  • Solar-powered pumps: Eco-friendly, off-grid operation
  • Jet pumps: For shallow wells (less than 25 meters)
  • Centrifugal pumps: For high-volume applications
  • Hand pumps: For basic needs without electricity

We recommend submersible pumps for most residential applications due to their efficiency, reliability, and quiet operation.

Can borehole water be used for drinking?

Borehole water can be safe for drinking after proper treatment:

  • Water testing: Essential to identify contaminants
  • Filtration systems: Remove sediment, bacteria, and particles
  • UV treatment: Kills microorganisms
  • Reverse osmosis: Removes dissolved solids and chemicals
  • Mineral balancing: Ensures healthy mineral content

We provide comprehensive water testing and recommend appropriate treatment systems based on the results. Never drink untreated borehole water.

What is borehole yield and how is it measured?

Borehole yield refers to the amount of water that can be sustainably extracted:

  • Measured in: Liters per hour (L/h) or cubic meters per day (m³/day)
  • Testing method: Pump test where water is extracted for 24+ hours
  • Average yields:
    • Residential: 1,000-5,000 L/h
    • Agricultural: 5,000-20,000 L/h
    • Commercial: 10,000-50,000 L/h

We conduct thorough yield testing to ensure your borehole meets your water requirements. Sustainable yield is crucial to prevent borehole depletion.

Maintenance Questions

What maintenance does a borehole require?

Regular maintenance ensures optimal performance:

  • Annual inspection: Full system check-up
  • Water testing: Every 6-12 months for quality
  • Pump servicing: Every 2-3 years
  • Filter replacement: Based on usage (3-12 months)
  • Borehole cleaning: If yield decreases significantly
  • Electrical checks: For pump controls and connections

We offer maintenance contracts starting at R1,500 per year for residential systems. Proper maintenance extends borehole life to 15-30+ years.

What are common borehole problems?

Common issues and their solutions:

  • Reduced yield: May need cleaning or deepening
  • Sand in water: Screen damage or pump too low
  • Pump failure: Electrical issues or wear
  • Water discoloration: Iron or manganese presence
  • Unusual noises: Pump or pipe issues
  • No water: Pump failure or borehole depletion

Most problems are preventable with regular maintenance. We offer 24/7 emergency repair services for urgent issues.

How long does a borehole last?

With proper maintenance:

  • Borehole structure: 15-50+ years
  • Submersible pump: 8-15 years
  • Casing: 20-50+ years (stainless steel)
  • Filtration system: 5-10 years (with filter changes)
  • Control systems: 10-15 years

The actual lifespan depends on water chemistry, usage patterns, and maintenance quality. Some boreholes in South Africa have been productive for over 50 years with proper care.
